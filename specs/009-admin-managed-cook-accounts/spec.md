# Feature Specification: Admin-Managed Cook Accounts

**Feature Branch**: `009-admin-managed-cook-accounts`

**Created**: 2026-06-15

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "Admin-managed COOK accounts — end-user login for the cook-facing recipe app, a
SEPARATE system from feature 008 (operator/dashboard accounts). Cooks can only log in (no self-signup);
a professional login UI on the widget; cook accounts are admin-provisioned and managed from the operator
dashboard; a logged-in cook OWNS their diet/allergy profile, favorites, and seen-history (replacing the
passwordless X-Profile-ID); the app is gated behind login. Enabled by constitution 2.0.0."

## Overview

Today every cook is **anonymous**: identity is a passwordless `X-Profile-ID` header generated by the
browser, and the chat app is open to anyone. That means a cook's saved favorites and stated diet/allergies
are tied to a disposable browser token, not to a person — clear the browser and they're gone, and there is
no way to grant or revoke access. This feature replaces that with **named cook accounts**: a cook **signs in**
to use the app, and their **profile (diet/allergies/servings), favorites, and seen-history belong to that
account**. Accounts are created and managed **only by an admin**; there is **no self-service sign-up**.

**Relationship to feature 008 (must not break it):** This is a **second, separate** account system. Feature
008 governs **operator/admin** access to the Streamlit **dashboard** and is unchanged. The admin who manages
cook accounts is an **008 admin**, acting from the dashboard. Cooks are a distinct identity domain that signs
in on the **React widget**. The two never share a credential store.

**Safety is unchanged.** The allergen wall, grounded retrieval, redaction, and the red-team/redaction gates
all still apply to every authenticated cook — gating the app behind login does not relax any safety rule.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-06-15

- Q: What identifies a cook after this change? → A: their authenticated **cook account**; the passwordless
  `X-Profile-ID` header is retired and the app is gated behind cook login (no anonymous access).
- Q: Is existing anonymous profile/favorites/seen-history data migrated to accounts? → A: No — those rows had
  no real owner; account-owned data starts fresh.
- Q: Cook session lifetime? → A: 8 hours (mirrors feature 008); a refresh stays signed in within that window.
- Q: Password recovery for cooks? → A: admin-only reset (mirrors 008); no self-service password change in v1.
- Q: How do the eval suites, CI smoke test, and the public demo reach the now-gated app? → A: a dedicated
  seeded **demo/eval cook account** (provisioned via the seed/bootstrap pipeline); CI and the live demo sign
  in as it. No anonymous bypass anywhere.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - A cook signs in and owns their data (Priority: P1)

A cook opens the app and is met with a professional login screen — there is no way to register. They sign in
with credentials an admin gave them and reach the recipe experience. Their **diet/allergies/servings**,
their **favorites**, and their **seen-history** are theirs: set once, they persist across sessions and
devices because they live with the account, not a browser token. Another cook signing in sees their *own*
data, never this cook's.

**Why this priority**: This is the whole point — a cook account that owns the cook's data. Without it the
accounts are meaningless. It is also the gating change: the app no longer serves anonymous users.

**Independent Test**: Seed one cook account, sign in via the login screen, set diet/allergies, save a
favorite, sign out and back in (and on a second browser) — the profile and favorite are still there; a
second seeded cook sees none of it.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a provisioned, active cook account, **When** the cook enters correct credentials on the login
   screen, **Then** they are signed in and the recipe app is shown.
2. **Given** a signed-in cook, **When** they set diet/allergies/servings and save a favorite, **Then** that
   profile and favorite are tied to their account and reappear after signing out and back in (including on a
   different device/browser).
3. **Given** two different cook accounts, **When** each signs in, **Then** each sees only their own
   favorites, profile, and seen-history — never the other's.
4. **Given** an unauthenticated visitor, **When** they try to use the app or its chat, **Then** access is
   refused until they sign in (no anonymous use), and no registration option is offered.

---

### User Story 2 - An admin provisions and manages cook accounts (Priority: P2)

From the operator dashboard, an admin creates a cook account (username, initial password), sees a list of
all cook accounts with their status, and can deactivate, reactivate, or reset the password of any account.
No self-service creation exists anywhere — a cook only gets in because an admin made them an account.

**Why this priority**: This is how access is granted and revoked. It is the only path by which a cook account
comes to exist, so it is required for US1 to be usable in production (though US1 is testable with a seeded
account).

**Independent Test**: As an 008 admin on the dashboard, create a cook account, confirm it appears in the
list, deactivate it, and reset a password — all from the dashboard; confirm no sign-up path exists.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an admin on the dashboard, **When** they submit a new cook username + initial password, **Then**
   the cook account is created and appears in the cook-accounts list as active.
2. **Given** an admin, **When** they deactivate a cook account, **Then** that cook can no longer sign in.
3. **Given** an admin, **When** they reset a cook's password, **Then** the new password works and the old one
   does not.
4. **Given** an admin, **When** they attempt to create a cook with an existing username, **Then** it is
   rejected with a clear message and no duplicate is created.

---

### User Story 3 - Revocation, persistence, and safety hold (Priority: P3)

A signed-in cook who refreshes the browser stays signed in. A deactivated cook is locked out promptly. And
every recipe a cook sees still respects their account's diet/allergies — the wall is enforced per
authenticated cook, exactly as before.

**Why this priority**: These are the continuity and safety properties around the core flows — important, but
they sit behind the sign-in-and-own-data and admin-provisioning flows.

**Independent Test**: Sign in, refresh → still signed in; deactivate the account → next action is denied;
sign in as a nut-allergic cook → no nut recipe is ever shown (the existing wall behavior, now keyed to the
account).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a signed-in cook, **When** they refresh the browser within the session window, **Then** they
   remain signed in.
2. **Given** a cook whose account was just deactivated, **When** they make their next request, **Then** access
   is denied (an existing session stops granting access).
3. **Given** a signed-in cook with a stated allergy, **When** they browse or plan, **Then** no recipe
   violating that allergy is ever shown (the wall holds per account).

---

### Edge Cases

- **No accounts yet**: with zero cook accounts, no one can use the app until an admin creates one — acceptable
  (the admin path exists via the dashboard / 008 bootstrap admin).
- **Legacy/anonymous request**: a request carrying the old `X-Profile-ID` (or none) is treated as
  unauthenticated and refused — the app is gated.
- **Duplicate cook username**: rejected (US2 #4).
- **Weak/empty password**: creating or resetting a password shorter than 8 characters is rejected.
- **Account enumeration**: a wrong password and an unknown username produce the same generic failure.
- **Data isolation**: a cook can never read or modify another cook's profile/favorites/seen-history.
- **Deactivated cook with a live session**: denied on the next request.
- **Existing anonymous data**: prior passwordless profiles/favorites/seen-history are not surfaced to any
  account (fresh start).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: A cook MUST sign in with a cook account before using the app; the chat/recipe experience MUST
  NOT serve unauthenticated (anonymous) users.
- **FR-002**: The app MUST present a professional login screen as its entry point, with NO self-service
  registration/sign-up path anywhere.
- **FR-003**: Cook accounts MUST be created only by an admin, and managed (list, deactivate, reactivate,
  reset password) only by an admin, from the operator dashboard.
- **FR-004**: A cook's diet/allergy/servings profile, favorites, and seen-history MUST be owned by (keyed to)
  the cook account, persisting across sessions and devices.
- **FR-005**: A cook MUST see only their own profile, favorites, and seen-history — never another cook's.
- **FR-006**: Cook usernames MUST be unique; creating a duplicate MUST be rejected without creating a second
  account.
- **FR-007**: The system MUST reject any cook password shorter than 8 characters (and empty), on both
  creation and admin reset; no other composition rules are imposed.
- **FR-008**: A sign-in attempt with incorrect credentials or an unknown username MUST fail with a single
  generic message that does not disclose whether the username exists.
- **FR-009**: An admin MUST be able to deactivate (disable) and reactivate (enable) a cook account;
  deactivation MUST prevent sign-in and revoke access on the next request.
- **FR-010**: An admin MUST be able to reset a cook account's password; there is no self-service password
  change in v1.
- **FR-011**: A signed-in cook MUST remain signed in across a browser refresh within the session window.
- **FR-012**: The passwordless `X-Profile-ID` identity MUST be retired; cook identity MUST come from the
  authenticated session, never from a request body or a client-supplied id header.
- **FR-013**: Cook accounts MUST be a separate identity domain from operator accounts (feature 008): a
  distinct account store and a distinct cook login surface (the widget); the two MUST NOT share a credential
  store.
- **FR-014**: Cook credentials MUST be stored durably; passwords MUST be stored only as irreversible (one-way)
  hashes.
- **FR-015**: Existing anonymous profile/favorites/seen-history data MUST NOT be migrated to accounts (the
  account-owned data starts fresh).
- **FR-016**: Admin actions on cook accounts (create, deactivate/reactivate, reset) MUST be recorded for
  audit, with no password/hash/secret written to logs or traces.
- **FR-017**: The allergen wall, grounded retrieval, redaction, and the red-team/redaction gates MUST
  continue to apply to every authenticated cook, unchanged.
- **FR-018**: Feature 008 (operator/dashboard accounts) MUST be unchanged by this feature; the dashboard's own
  login is unaffected.
- **FR-019**: The feature MUST preserve the stack guarantees — hosted-only inference, no `torch` in any image,
  secrets (the session signing key) only in the secret store.
- **FR-020**: The system MUST provide a **seeded demo/eval cook account** (admin-provisioned via the
  seed/bootstrap pipeline) that the automated eval suites, the CI smoke test, and the public live demo sign
  in as. There MUST be no anonymous/test bypass of the login gate — CI and the demo authenticate as a real
  account, so the gate is exercised exactly as in production.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Cook Account**: a named login identity for the cook-facing app. Key attributes: unique username, display
  name, status (active | disabled), an irreversible password hash, audit timestamps, and which admin created
  it. Has no internal role (all are cooks); the managing authority is an operator/admin (feature 008).
  Replaces the passwordless profile-ID as the cook's identity.
- **Cook Profile**: the cook's diet, allergies, and default servings — the constraints that drive the wall —
  now owned by the Cook Account (previously keyed to the passwordless profile-ID).
- **Favorite**: a saved recipe, owned by the Cook Account; persists across sessions/devices.
- **Seen-History**: the per-cook record of shown recipes (drives freshness), owned by the Cook Account.
- **Cook Session**: the authenticated state established at sign-in that survives a browser refresh within a
  bounded window and ends on sign-out, expiry, or account deactivation.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: A signed-in cook's profile and favorites persist across sign-out/sign-in and across a different
  browser/device 100% of the time (they are account-owned, not browser-bound).
- **SC-002**: There are **zero** anonymous access paths to the app — every chat/recipe action requires a
  valid cook session.
- **SC-003**: There are **zero** self-service registration entry points anywhere.
- **SC-004**: One cook account can access **zero** of another cook account's favorites/profile/seen-history.
- **SC-005**: An admin can create a working cook account in under 2 minutes, and the cook can sign in on the
  first attempt.
- **SC-006**: A deactivated cook account is denied access on 100% of subsequent attempts.
- **SC-007**: A cook who refreshes the page remains signed in within the session window 100% of the time.
- **SC-008**: The allergen wall and grounding show **no** regression — the red-team and redaction gates stay
  green with cook identity now account-based, and feature 008's behavior is unchanged.

## Assumptions

- **Separate system, shared primitives.** Cook accounts are their own store and login surface (the widget),
  distinct from operator accounts (008). They may reuse the same underlying authentication primitives, but the
  two identity domains never share a credential store (FR-013).
- **Admin authority comes from 008.** The admin who manages cook accounts is an operator account with the
  admin role (feature 008), acting from the dashboard. This feature adds cook-account management to that
  existing admin surface; it does not introduce a new admin system.
- **Authentication is username + password.** No SSO, OAuth, MFA, or social login in v1.
- **Session window is 8 hours**, mirroring feature 008; a refresh stays signed in within that window.
- **Cook accounts have no internal role** — every cook account is simply a cook. Role-based distinctions live
  only in the operator/admin domain (008).
- **No data migration.** Pre-existing anonymous profiles/favorites/seen-history are not carried into accounts;
  account-owned data starts fresh (FR-015).
- **Admin-only password reset** is the recovery path; self-service password change is deferred to a future
  iteration.
- **Gating is total.** There is no anonymous/guest mode; the public chat endpoint now requires a valid cook
  session. This is permitted by constitution 2.0.0 (admin-provisioned end-user accounts; no self-signup).
- **CI/demo authenticate as a seeded cook (FR-020).** Rather than a test bypass, a dedicated demo/eval cook
  account is seeded so the eval suites, CI smoke test, and the live demo log in like a real user — keeping
  the gate honest and the red-team/redaction gates exercised against the authenticated path. The 007 demo
  scenario + RUNBOOK will be updated to sign in as this account (a downstream doc follow-up).

## Dependencies

- **Feature 008** (operator/admin accounts + dashboard auth) — supplies the admin identity and the dashboard
  surface from which cook accounts are managed.
- The **cook-facing widget** and the **public chat** path (both gated behind cook login by this feature).
- The project's **primary datastore** (durable cook accounts + re-keying profile/favorites/seen-history to
  the account) and its migration mechanism.
- The project's **secret store** (the session signing key).
- **Constitution 2.0.0**, which permits admin-provisioned end-user authentication and the retirement of the
  passwordless profile-ID.
